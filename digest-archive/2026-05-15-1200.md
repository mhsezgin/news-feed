## [law] Supreme Court blocks Fifth Circuit order, preserves mifepristone access by mail while Louisiana lawsuit proceeds

**Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/05/court-allows-for-access-to-abortion-pill-by-mail-for-now/)

**Lead:** The Supreme Court on May 14 granted a stay of the Fifth Circuit's order that would have reinstated in-person dispensing requirements for mifepristone, preserving nationwide mail-order and telehealth access to the abortion pill while Louisiana's lawsuit against the FDA continues in the lower courts, with Justices Thomas and Alito dissenting.

**Context:** Louisiana filed suit last fall asking federal courts to reinstate the in-person dispensing requirement that the FDA relaxed in 2021, allowing mifepristone to be prescribed via telehealth and sent by mail or dispensed at retail pharmacies. A three-judge Fifth Circuit panel sided with Louisiana on May 1, 2026, temporarily reinstating the in-person requirement. Justice Samuel Alito, who handles emergency applications from the Fifth Circuit, issued a series of administrative stays pausing that order — first through May 11, then extended through May 14 — while Danco Laboratories and GenBioPro's emergency applications were before the full Court. On May 14, the full Court granted the stay, with only Justices Thomas and Alito dissenting publicly; Alito wrote separately calling the majority order "unreasoned" and "remarkable." The case now returns to the Fifth Circuit for expedited proceedings on the merits of Louisiana's claim that its sovereignty interest entitles it to impose prescribing requirements on an FDA-approved drug beyond what federal law mandates.

**Why it matters:** This is the second major Supreme Court proceeding over mifepristone in two years: the 2024 ruling in FDA v. Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine dismissed the prior challenge on standing grounds, but the Court's reasoning explicitly left open the possibility that states with direct regulatory interests could bring their own suits, which Louisiana then did. The current case presents a major preemption question about whether states can impose prescribing restrictions on FDA-approved medications beyond what federal law requires — a question with implications well beyond abortion medication for any federally approved drug that states might seek to restrict independently. The Trump administration's ongoing internal safety review of mifepristone adds a parallel track: if FDA reinstates restrictions administratively, it could moot the litigation, but any such action would face its own legal challenge. If the Fifth Circuit rules for Louisiana on the merits, the Supreme Court will take the case with full briefing and likely resolve the federal-state preemption question before the 2026 elections — a politically significant timeline given the issue's electoral salience in states with tight Senate and House races.

## [law] Republican legislators urge Supreme Court not to intervene in Virginia redistricting fight

**Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/05/republican-legislators-urge-justices-to-leave-virginia-supreme-courts-redistricting-ruling-in-pl/)

**Lead:** Republican members of Virginia's legislature filed a May 14 brief urging the Supreme Court not to stay the Virginia Supreme Court's ruling that invalidated a mid-cycle redistricting amendment on procedural grounds, arguing that Democrats waived any federal constitutional claims by failing to raise them during the state court proceedings.

**Context:** In April 2026, Virginia voters approved by roughly 3 percentage points a constitutional amendment allowing the General Assembly to redraw congressional maps outside the decennial cycle. The Virginia Supreme Court invalidated the referendum on May 8, finding the General Assembly violated state constitutional procedures: amendments must pass in two separate legislative sessions separated by a House of Delegates election, but the General Assembly voted on the amendment after voters had already begun casting early ballots in the 2025 election. Governor Spanberger announced Virginia would not use the new map in 2026. Attorney General Jones filed an emergency application with the US Supreme Court to stay the ruling and allow the more Democratic-favorable map. Republican legislators filed a response on May 14 arguing federal courts should not intervene because Democrats failed to preserve any federal constitutional claims in the state proceedings, and separately that state courts applying state amendment-ratification procedures do not present a reviewable federal question.

**Why it matters:** The procedural forfeiture argument is potentially dispositive: if the Supreme Court agrees Democrats did not preserve federal claims below, it can deny the stay without reaching the merits — a much faster resolution. The timing is acute because Virginia election officials need a final congressional map by mid-May to begin ballot preparation. The underlying substantive question — whether a state court's application of its own amendment-ratification rules can ever present a federal constitutional issue — is unusual and sits at the intersection of federalism doctrine and elections law. The case illustrates how redistricting litigation has become a permanent feature of the Court's emergency docket, with parties on both sides using emergency applications to lock in maps before elections without full briefing.

## [law] Australia's High Court hears first-ever climate case: coal mine scope-3 emissions

**Source:** [Verfassungsblog](https://verfassungsblog.de/climate-change-in-the-high-court-of-australia/)

**Lead:** On May 13, Australia's High Court heard the country's first-ever climate change case, weighing whether the approval of the Mount Pleasant coal mine expansion was unlawful because it failed to assess the local climate impacts of the mine's scope-3 emissions — the greenhouse gases released when exported coal is burned overseas — which represent an estimated 98% of the project's total lifetime greenhouse output.

**Context:** The case is MACH Energy Australia v. DAMS HEG, brought by a Hunter Valley community group challenging the 2023 approval of the Mount Pleasant open-cut coal mine expansion in NSW. At issue is whether NSW's Environmental Planning and Assessment Act required regulators to consider scope-3 emissions — those released when the exported coal is burned by overseas buyers. The NSW Court of Appeal ruled unanimously in July 2025 that the approval was unlawful, finding planning authorities are legally required to consider local climate impacts of downstream emissions. MACH Energy appealed. The High Court heard arguments May 13 on whether scope-3 emissions constitute an environmental impact "in the locality" under the statutory language.

**Why it matters:** If the High Court upholds the NSW Court of Appeal, it will establish that Australian planning law mandates full lifecycle emissions analysis for fossil fuel approvals, forcing reassessment of pending coal and gas projects worth tens of billions and raising the bar for any new fossil fuel development. A reversal would signal that diffuse global climate effects cannot satisfy a "in the locality" threshold, potentially slowing analogous proceedings in the UK, Netherlands, Germany, and the US. The ICJ's recent advisory opinion establishing state obligations to prevent climate harm provides additional context in which the High Court is deciding.

## [cybersecurity] TanStack npm supply chain attack breaches OpenAI and Mistral codebases

**Source:** [The Record](https://therecord.media/openai-asks-macos-users-to-update-tanstack-npm)

**Lead:** A supply chain attack poisoned 84 npm package artifacts in TanStack — an open-source JavaScript ecosystem with over 12 million weekly downloads — compromising two OpenAI employee Mac devices and Mistral AI's codebase management system in two attack phases on April 29 and May 11, with threat actor TeamPCP exfiltrating signing certificates and credential material before OpenAI detected and contained the incident.

**Context:** TeamPCP injected malware into 84 TanStack npm artifacts in two waves, designed to steal credentials and self-propagate to other packages maintained by compromised developers. OpenAI confirmed two employee Mac devices were breached, affecting limited iOS, macOS, and Windows product repositories; the company found no evidence of customer data theft. Mistral confirmed its codebase management system was also affected. The attacker targeted application signing certificates — the credentials macOS uses to verify software authenticity. OpenAI isolated affected systems, rotated all credentials, reviewed software notarizations, and is asking macOS users who haven't updated ChatGPT since April 2026 to update by June 12 or lose support. TeamPCP had previously attacked LiteLLM and the European Commission.

**Why it matters:** Supply chain attacks via package registries reach millions of downstream projects instantly. Theft of signing certificates is especially consequential for macOS, enabling distribution of malicious apps that appear legitimate to both users and security tools. The involvement of OpenAI and Mistral in the same attack by the same threat actor suggests specific targeting of AI companies' code and tooling. TeamPCP's prior European Commission targeting adds a geopolitical dimension relevant to EU Cyber Resilience Act and US AI security framework discussions.

Techdirt identifies three strategic motivations: compliance costs are manageable for incumbents but devastating for small AI startups; backing social media regulation diverts attention from AI-specific bills; and OpenAI faces litigation from families alleging ChatGPT companion products contributed to teen suicides.

**Why it matters:** OpenAI's endorsement gives KOSA significant new momentum as it is reintroduced with growing corporate support. The regulatory capture dynamic matters because incumbent-endorsed regulation tends to be designed around the incumbent's compliance infrastructure, entrenching it while generating collateral costs for users and small competitors. The populations most likely to be harmed are those the law's supporters invoke: LGBTQ+ teenagers, minors seeking mental health information, and young people without government ID.

## [tech law] State age-verification bills threaten Linux distributions with impossible compliance demands

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/930573/age-verification-bills-linux-open-source)

**Lead:** A wave of US state laws mandating operating-system-level age verification — advancing in Louisiana, Illinois, Texas, Utah, and New York, plus the federal Parents Decide Act — is creating an existential compliance challenge for Linux distributions, which lack the centralized user databases and corporate revenue to implement the identity-check systems the bills require.

**Context:** Laws in Louisiana (effective July 2026), Illinois, Texas, Utah, and New York require OS providers to verify user ages at setup via financial records, government ID, or app-store accounts, and share data via API. The federal Parents Decide Act mandates the same. Linux distributions — volunteer communities with no centralized user databases — cannot implement these systems. MX Linux, Zorin OS, Debian, Parrot OS, and Garuda Linux all refused to implement age checks in April-May 2026 statements; some may block US IP ranges instead. Colorado's bill was amended to exempt open source after System76 CEO Richell worked with Senator Ball, but no such exemptions exist elsewhere.

**Why it matters:** The bills assume all software is delivered through centralized app stores with identity-linked accounts — a compliance architecture that doesn't exist in open source. If the federal Parents Decide Act passes without an open-source exemption, Linux distributions would technically need to build systems they lack resources to construct. The practical outcome — blocking US IP ranges — would reduce access to privacy-preserving computing for journalists, activists, abuse survivors, and security researchers. The bills also create unexamined collateral damage to open-source infrastructure underlying the internet's server and developer tooling.

## [tech law] Congress narrows GUARD Act to AI companions but serious problems remain

**Source:** [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2026/05/14/congress-narrowed-the-guard-act-but-serious-problems-remain/)

**Lead:** An amended GUARD Act released in May 2026 narrows the AI age-restriction bill from all AI chatbots to AI companion applications presenting a persistent persona or simulating emotional relationships, but retains mandatory real-world identity verification and increases per-violation penalties from $100,000 to $250,000, enforceable by both federal and state officials.

**Context:** The original GUARD Act potentially covered all AI-powered conversational tools. The amended version limits scope to systems presenting a "persistent identity, persona or character" or "interactions involving emotional disclosures." Despite this narrowing, the bill retains "reasonable age verification" requiring financial records, government ID, or app-store accounts. The per-violation fine rose to $250,000, jointly enforceable by FTC and state AGs. Critics note the "emotional disclosure" and "persistent persona" definitions may cover customer service chatbots that express sympathy or remember preferences, meaning scope is wider than intended. The EFF argues steep penalties plus vague definitions will push small developers to block all underage users rather than navigate uncertain legal standards.

**Why it matters:** The GUARD Act exemplifies how AI regulation bills narrow stated scope but preserve consequential enforcement mechanisms — identity verification mandates and steep penalties — that drive compliance behavior regardless of definitional scope. The $250,000 per-violation structure creates de facto market consolidation favoring incumbents. Mandatory real-world identity verification creates a novel high-value surveillance dataset whose breach risk and misuse potential have not been publicly analyzed. Vague definitions guarantee years of enforcement litigation before actual scope is known — a poor outcome for small developers assessing compliance risk.

## [ai] Meta broadly rolls out neural EMG handwriting for Ray-Ban Display glasses

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/930941/meta-ray-ban-display-virtual-neural-handwriting-apps-developer)

**Lead:** Meta has begun broadly rolling out neural EMG (electromyography) handwriting support to all Ray-Ban Display glasses owners, allowing users to type messages by tracing letters on any flat surface while the wrist-worn neural band detects the underlying muscle signals in real time and converts them into digital text, marking the first commercial deployment of EMG-based text input at consumer scale.

**Context:** Meta's Ray-Ban Display glasses launched with a limited developer preview of the neural EMG band at CES 2026 in January. The broad rollout makes the feature available to all current glasses and neural band owners. The EMG wristband detects electrical signals generated by hand and forearm muscles: when a user traces a letter on any surface, the band reads distinct muscle activation patterns for each finger position and converts them into character input without requiring a touchscreen, keyboard, or voice command. The system processes signals in real time and adapts to individual writing styles. The feature integrates with Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, and native iOS and Android messaging apps. Meta also enabled teleprompter functionality — projecting text into the glasses' field of view — broadly with this rollout.

**Why it matters:** This is the first mainstream consumer deployment of EMG-based text input, and the broad rollout will generate real-world accuracy and adoption data informing the next generation of neural interface design across the industry. For Apple, which acquired EMG startup Proxima in 2024 and is expected to include similar capabilities in its AR glasses roadmap, Meta's successful consumer-scale execution compresses the response timeline. For privacy researchers and regulators, the neural band continuously monitors muscle signals even when not actively composing text, capturing physiological data — hand tremors, stress indicators, involuntary muscle activity — beyond intentional inputs; Meta has not publicly specified its neural signal data practices or whether the data trains Meta AI models. The feature also has accessibility implications for users with conditions impairing keyboard or touchscreen interaction.
