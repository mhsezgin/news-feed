## [law] Supreme Court rules 8-1 in Havana Docks: cruise lines liable under Helms-Burton Act for using confiscated Cuban port

**Source:** <https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/05/court-rules-against-cruise-lines-in-cuban-confiscation-case/>

**LEAD:** The Supreme Court ruled 8-1 on May 21 that Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian, and MSC cruise lines are liable under the Helms-Burton Act for using Havana's port facilities — confiscated from the American-owned Havana Docks Corporation by Cuba's Castro government in 1960 — potentially restoring a district court award of over $400 million in damages.

**CONTEXT:** Havana Docks Corporation held the contractual right to operate the docks at Havana's harbor until Cuba nationalized the port in 1960. Under Title III of the 1996 Cuban Liberty and Democratic Solidarity Act — the Helms-Burton Act — U.S. nationals with claims to confiscated property may sue anyone who "traffics" in that property. Between 2016 and 2019, when President Trump ended a prior-administration suspension of Title III suits, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, Carnival, and MSC collectively transported nearly one million cruise passengers through the Havana terminal. U.S. District Judge Beth Bloom in Miami found all four lines liable and awarded Havana Docks over $400 million total; the Eleventh Circuit reversed 2-1, reasoning that Havana Docks' operational rights had expired in 2004. Justice Thomas, writing for eight justices, held that confiscated property becomes permanently "tainted — off limits," and that the statute covers both the plaintiff's expired interest and the physical asset itself, meaning any user of the physical property faces liability regardless of whether the plaintiff's specific contractual right had lapsed. Justice Kagan dissented alone.

**WHY IT MATTERS:** The ruling dramatically expands Helms-Burton Title III by untethering liability from the specific nature or duration of the plaintiff's confiscated interest. Any business using Cuban assets with a pre-1960 confiscation history can face claims from any prior American interest-holder, even if that interest expired decades before the defendant's use began. With Trump having lifted the Helms-Burton suspension in 2019, the decision activates a dormant liability regime covering billions of dollars of Cuban assets. The case returns to lower courts for remaining defenses, but the potential damages — trebled under Helms-Burton — make this one of the most consequential property-confiscation decisions in years. European governments, particularly Spain, may seek diplomatic or WTO channels to contain the ruling's impact on non-US defendants.

## [law] SCOTUS approaching end of term: birthright citizenship, independent agency firings, and transgender sports bans all due by late June

**Source:** <https://www.cbsnews.com/news/supreme-court-major-cases-2026/>

**LEAD:** The Supreme Court must issue final opinions on its most consequential cases by the end of June, including Trump's birthright citizenship executive order, his firing of FTC and other independent-agency members without statutory cause, and two state laws banning transgender athletes from girls' and women's sports teams — any one of which could reshape federal law for a generation.

**CONTEXT:** In Trump v. Barbara, the Court is reviewing Trump's first-day executive order attempting to end birthright citizenship — Fourteenth Amendment protection the administration argues applies only to children of citizens and legal permanent residents — with a majority of justices appearing skeptical at argument. In Trump v. Slaughter, Trump fired FTC Commissioner Rebecca Slaughter without the "cause" required by the FTC's enabling statute, directly challenging the 1935 precedent Humphrey's Executor v. United States, which authorized Congress to insulate agency boards from at-will presidential removal. The transgender-athlete cases consolidate West Virginia H.B. 3293 and Idaho H.B. 500, with a majority appearing at argument to uphold the bans. The Court also placed the Dershowitz v. CNN petition — asking the Court to revisit the "actual malice" standard from New York Times v. Sullivan (1964) — on its May 21 conference for a cert vote.

**WHY IT MATTERS:** The birthright citizenship case could be the most consequential immigration ruling in over a century; upholding Trump's order would affect an estimated 150,000 births per year. Overturning Humphrey's Executor in the Slaughter case would give the president full removal authority over the FTC, SEC, NLRB, and every other multimember commission, making all regulatory agencies subject to political control. The transgender sports cases would resolve a circuit split affecting 27 existing state laws, foreclosing Title IX challenges across all 50 states. And if the Court grants cert in Dershowitz v. CNN, it opens the door to reconsidering the 62-year-old actual-malice standard that has shielded American media from defamation liability.

**Source:** <https://therecord.media/canadian-man-arrested-charged-running-kimwolf-botnet>

**LEAD:** Canadian authorities arrested Jacob Butler, 23, in Ottawa on charges of running the KimWolf DDoS-for-hire service, which infected over one million devices worldwide, issued more than 25,000 attack commands reaching a peak of nearly 30 terabits per second, and targeted at least one Department of Defense IP block — with a US extradition request already filed and a joint operation simultaneously seizing 45 other DDoS-for-hire platforms.

**CONTEXT:** KimWolf operated as a booter or stresser service — an illegal DDoS-for-hire platform that rents attack capacity to paying customers. Butler, operating under the handle "Dort," was publicly identified by security journalist Brian Krebs in February 2026. KimWolf built its attack infrastructure by compromising consumer devices — particularly digital photo frames and web cameras — and enrolling them in a botnet rented to cybercriminals. At peak capacity the botnet could generate nearly 30 terabits per second of attack traffic, one of the largest DDoS volumes ever measured. Victims lost between hundreds of thousands and millions of dollars each per incident; at least one attack explicitly targeted Department of Defense IP addresses. The US Justice Department filed an extradition warrant; Butler faces up to ten years in federal prison on one count of aiding and abetting computer intrusion. The arrest resulted from a coordinated March 2026 operation involving US, Canadian, and German law enforcement and cybersecurity firms, which simultaneously seized infrastructure supporting 45 other DDoS-for-hire platforms.

**WHY IT MATTERS:** A verified 30-Tbps attack capacity from consumer IoT hardware illustrates how dramatically the DDoS threat landscape has shifted — camera and picture-frame botnets can now generate volumes that a decade ago required compromised datacenter infrastructure. The DoD targeting elevates this case from routine cybercrime toward national-security territory, and extraditing a Canadian for crimes against US government systems is legally uncommon. The simultaneous seizure of 45 other platforms is the broadest coordinated booter-market disruption in years; its lasting impact depends on how many seizures result in prosecutions versus temporary displacement. The case arrives as CISA finalizes CIRCIA rules requiring 72-hour breach notification from critical-infrastructure operators — attacks of this type would now trigger mandatory federal disclosure, increasing legal exposure for victim organizations.
