## [tech law] Federal judge permanently bars DOGE from reinstating ChatGPT-driven cuts to $100M in NEH humanities grants

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/policy/927071/doge-chatgpt-grants-cuts-ai-illegal)

**Lead:** US District Judge Colleen McMahon on May 8 permanently barred the Trump administration from reinstating DOGE's termination of roughly 1,400 National Endowment for the Humanities grants, ruling in a 143-page opinion that DOGE staffers' use of ChatGPT and DEI keyword lists to identify grants for elimination constituted unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination under the First and Fifth Amendments.

**Context:** Between April 1 and April 3, 2025, two DOGE staffers — Justin Fox and Nate Cavanaugh — eliminated roughly 1,400 NEH grants collectively worth more than $100 million. Depositions revealed that Fox copied and pasted ChatGPT-generated "DEI rationales" into spreadsheets, using keyword searches for terms including "DEI," "Equity," "Inclusion," "BIPOC," and "LGBTQ" to flag grants for termination. Projects cut included an anthology of short fiction by Jewish writers from the Soviet Union about Holocaust survival, Native American oral history archives, African American history projects, and women's studies grants. Judge McMahon found that the NEH grants officer who formally approved the terminations lacked the legal authority to cancel them, and that even if she had, the mechanism was unconstitutionally discriminatory because government funding cannot be conditioned on viewpoint. McMahon devoted an entire section of her opinion to rejecting the administration's "ChatGPT defense," invoking comedian Flip Wilson's "the Devil made me do it" line to note that the AI chatbot's role did not insulate the government from constitutional accountability. The ruling was not accompanied by a stay, meaning the grant terminations remain permanently blocked pending any appeal.

**Why it matters:** The ruling is the most detailed judicial accounting yet of how AI tools can be used by government actors in discriminatory ways, and McMahon's logic — that deploying an AI chatbot to execute a discriminatory purpose does not make the discrimination any less constitutional — will become a critical precedent for future challenges to AI-assisted government decision-making across any agency that uses automated systems to allocate, terminate, or restrict government benefits. The First Amendment viewpoint discrimination finding is particularly potent: it means any future administration that uses content-based keywords to cut funding to disfavored academic or cultural work will face the same constitutional bar, regardless of what technology is used to identify targets. The ruling also signals that courts will scrutinize not just the formal decision-maker but the actual operative process, since DOGE staffers acted far outside their delegated authority. If the administration appeals to the Second Circuit, it faces an unfavorable record given how explicitly McMahon documented the discriminatory mechanics from deposition testimony, making the path to reversal narrow.

## [global politics] Trump-Xi summit day two: Xi warns Taiwan misstep will put US-China into 'great jeopardy,' both sides agree on Hormuz openness and lift rare-earths export freeze

**Source:** [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/14/trump-xi-beijing-summit-trade-taiwan-ai-iran-rare-earths-tariffs.html)

**Lead:** At their two-day Beijing summit, Chinese President Xi Jinping warned Donald Trump directly that mishandling Taiwan would put the US-China relationship in "great jeopardy" with real risk of "clashes and even conflicts," while both leaders announced trade outcomes including China lifting its rare-earths export freeze and a joint commitment to keeping the Strait of Hormuz open amid the Iran war.

**Context:** Trump arrived in Beijing on May 13 for his first visit to China as president since 2017 — the first time a sitting US president has visited China in nearly a decade — accompanied by a delegation including Apple CEO Tim Cook, Tesla CEO Elon Musk, and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, signaling the visit's economic dimension. The summit focused on four issues: trade and tariffs, Taiwan, the Iran war, and artificial intelligence. On trade, Xi said "our economic and trade teams produced generally balanced and positive outcomes," with China agreeing to halt its export restrictions on rare earths — materials critical for semiconductors, electric vehicles, and defense systems — that had been deployed as leverage in tariff negotiations. On Taiwan, Xi's warning was the clearest he has publicly delivered to a US president: he invoked the "Thucydides Trap" (the historical pattern of conflict between rising and ruling powers) and said Taiwan was the single most important issue and that if not "handled properly" the bilateral relationship would reach a "dangerous place." The joint statement, notably, contains no mention of Taiwan — China's insistence on outcome language that avoids the word entirely. On the Iran war, both leaders agreed the Strait of Hormuz must remain open to commercial shipping; Xi's involvement marks the first time China has formally weighed in on the conflict's key chokepoint.

**Why it matters:** Xi's Taiwan warning is the most consequential public statement to emerge from any US-China summit in years: it was delivered directly to Trump in Beijing, not through a communiqué or diplomatic channel, which means Trump cannot later claim ignorance of the red line China considers existential. The lifting of the rare-earths freeze has immediate industrial significance — the export restrictions had begun affecting semiconductor fabrication timelines, battery supply chains, and US defense procurement for systems including F-35 components and guided munitions that rely on neodymium and dysprosium. The Hormuz agreement is significant for the Iran war: it implicitly acknowledges China's role as a mediator or at least a floor-setter for conflict boundaries, giving Beijing a formal stake in keeping its own oil supply lines intact. The notable absence of Taiwan in the joint statement means the two sides likely papered over their deepest disagreement, leaving the same structural tension intact. The presence of Musk, Cook, and Huang in the delegation raises inevitable questions about corporate access and influence in diplomatic settings, particularly given Musk's dual role as Trump advisor and Tesla CEO with major China manufacturing exposure.

## [global politics] Latvian PM Evika Silina resigns as coalition collapses over government's handling of Ukrainian drones that crashed into Latvian territory

**Source:** [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwy21k5917jo)

**Lead:** Latvian Prime Minister Evika Silina announced her resignation on May 14 after the coalition government's left-wing Progressives party withdrew its support, triggering the collapse of her parliamentary majority following a crisis over the government's response to at least two suspected Ukrainian drones that entered Latvian airspace and struck a fuel storage facility on May 7.

**Context:** On May 7, two suspected Ukrainian drones crossed into Latvian airspace — one striking an empty fuel storage facility — in what Ukraine's Foreign Ministry attributed to Russian electronic warfare deliberately diverting Ukrainian drones off their Russian targets. Latvia is a NATO member and one of the countries that has provided the most proportional military support to Ukraine. The drone incursion immediately exposed fault lines within the centre-right coalition: Defence Minister Andris Spruds resigned by May 11 after his handling of the airspace violations came under fire, and the Progressives party then announced it was withdrawing from the coalition entirely, leaving PM Silina of the New Unity party without a parliamentary majority. Silina, who had led the government since 2023, announced her resignation May 14, triggering the collapse of the entire cabinet with a general election already scheduled for October 2026. The incident puts Latvia — a small state of 1.8 million people bordering Russia — at the center of one of the most politically sensitive aspects of Western support for Ukraine: the risk that Ukrainian military operations may unintentionally violate the airspace or territory of NATO allies, creating alliance complications even among Ukraine's strongest supporters.

**Why it matters:** Latvia's government collapse over this incident reveals a structural tension that every NATO ally bordering Ukraine or Russia must manage: how to publicly support Ukrainian military operations while also holding Ukraine accountable for errant strikes or airspace violations on allied territory. The political implications go beyond Latvia — similar drone overflights have occurred in Romania and other NATO states, and each time the host government must choose between absorbing domestic political costs or confronting Ukraine diplomatically. The October 2026 election timing means Latvia will be governed by a caretaker administration during one of the most critical periods of the war, potentially affecting its foreign policy coordination. Defense Minister Spruds's resignation and subsequent government collapse also send a signal to other NATO defense establishments about the domestic political liability of intelligence gaps regarding Ukrainian weapons trajectories. The incident could strengthen arguments within some alliance members for better coordination between NATO's air picture and Ukrainian military targeting, a conversation that has been politically difficult to have openly given the desire to avoid any appearance of constraining Ukraine's war effort.

## [global politics] Nigerian court sentences former Power Minister Saleh Mamman to 75 years in absentia for laundering ₦33.8 billion earmarked for hydroelectric projects

**Source:** [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c0j296392j1o)

**Lead:** Nigeria's Federal High Court on May 13 sentenced former Minister of Power Saleh Mamman to a cumulative 75-year prison term in absentia after convicting him on all 12 counts of money laundering and indirect conversion of ₦33.8 billion in public funds earmarked for the Mambilla and Zungeru hydroelectric projects during the Buhari administration.

**Context:** Justice James Omotosho of the Federal High Court in Abuja, acting on prosecution brought by Nigeria's Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), convicted Mamman — who served as Minister of Power between 2019 and 2021 under former President Muhammadu Buhari — on all 12 counts related to the diversion of funds allocated to two major national infrastructure projects: the Mambilla hydroelectric project (a long-delayed 3,050 MW dam on the Taraba River, one of Africa's most ambitious power projects) and the Zungeru hydroelectric dam (700 MW, located in Niger State). Justice Omotosho ordered the sentences on each of the 12 counts to run consecutively rather than concurrently, transforming individual terms of approximately six years per count into a cumulative 75-year sentence. Mamman was not present in court for sentencing, and his current whereabouts were not confirmed at the time of judgment. He becomes the first minister who served under former President Buhari to be jailed for corruption, a notable milestone given the scale of corruption allegations that surrounded the 2015–2023 Buhari era despite the administration's anti-corruption rhetoric.

**Why it matters:** The conviction is genuinely rare for Nigeria's anti-corruption framework: previous high-profile cases against senior Buhari-era officials either stalled, ended in acquittals, or resulted in plea bargains with minimal custodial sentences. The consecutive sentencing — an unusual judicial choice — appears designed to signal that the court viewed the 12-count scheme as an aggravated pattern of conduct warranting punishment beyond what concurrent sentences would convey, and to maximize deterrence for ministerial-level corruption. The Mambilla project specifically has been plagued by contractor disputes and funding scandals for decades; the conviction adds legal accountability to what has been an emblematic failure of Nigerian infrastructure planning. The in absentia nature of the verdict introduces immediate enforcement complexity: Mamman must be located and extradited or surrendered before the sentence can be served, and Nigeria's record of executing foreign extradition requests is mixed. The EFCC's success in this case may signal a more aggressive posture under the current Tinubu administration toward prosecuting predecessors' officials, though critics will note that the political incentive to pursue Buhari-era figures is different from the political incentive to pursue allies of the current government.

## [ai] Netflix quietly launches INKubator, a GenAI-native animation studio led by ex-DreamWorks and A24 executive, aiming to produce short-form AI-generated content

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/column/930118/netflix-gen-ai-animation-inkubator)

**Lead:** Netflix has quietly launched INKubator, an internal animation studio built from the ground up as a "GenAI-native" production unit that quietly began operations in March 2026 under Serrena Iyer — a former strategy and operations executive at DreamWorks Animation, MRC Studios, and A24 — with a mandate to bridge generative AI with short-form animated storytelling, according to job listings and LinkedIn profiles reviewed by reporters.

**Context:** INKubator is described in Netflix job postings as "our next-generation, creative-led, GenAI-native animation studio" designed to "bridge innovation with imaginative storytelling." The studio launched in March 2026 but has not been publicly announced by Netflix, with its existence inferred by reporters from job listings and LinkedIn profiles of staff. Serrena Iyer, who has held senior operational roles at DreamWorks Animation, MRC Studios, and A24 Films, leads the unit. The INKubator initiative builds on Netflix's already-documented use of AI in live-action production: co-CEO Ted Sarandos confirmed the platform used AI-generated footage in the Argentine science fiction series El Eternauta — specifically a building-collapse scene completed ten times faster and at lower cost than equivalent traditional VFX work — which Sarandos described as "the very first GenAI final footage to appear on screen" on the platform. INKubator represents an escalation from supplemental AI VFX to a studio whose entire production pipeline is built around generative AI tools from development through delivery. Netflix has not disclosed which AI vendors or models INKubator is using, nor whether any union agreements govern the studio's AI-generated workflows.

**Why it matters:** INKubator represents the first major studio's attempt to build an entirely GenAI-native production pipeline rather than adding AI tools onto legacy workflows — a structural distinction that matters enormously for the ongoing labor negotiations with animation unions, since a ground-up AI studio has no legacy workforce to retrain or reassign. Netflix's choice to launch quietly and without announcement is itself revealing: the studio is clearly aware that a public announcement of a "replace animators with AI" initiative would provoke significant union response, and is instead building facts on the ground before the conversation becomes unavoidable. Short-form content is the ideal testing ground for this model because the financial stakes per project are lower and the creative iteration cycles are faster, giving Netflix real performance data before committing to AI-native feature-length production. The scale question matters enormously: if INKubator can produce short-form content at meaningful cost and quality reductions, Netflix faces a board-level argument for dramatically expanding the model into feature animation — a segment where Netflix has invested billions in traditional studio deals (with DreamWorks, Sony Pictures Animation, and others) that would be directly threatened by internal AI production. The SAG-AFTRA and Animation Guild contracts negotiated through 2025 contain AI use provisions, but "GenAI-native" studios that have never employed traditional animators may not be covered by those provisions at all.
