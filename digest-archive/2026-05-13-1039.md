## [law] Court addresses one redistricting battle, faces another: Alabama cleared, Virginia files emergency application

**Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/05/court-addresses-one-redistricting-battle-faces-another/)

**Lead:** The Supreme Court on Monday cleared Alabama to use a congressional map a lower court found racially discriminatory, while Virginia Democrats simultaneously filed an emergency application to reinstate a Democratic-drawn map that could flip four House seats before the November 2026 elections.

**Context:** Monday, May 12 was a double redistricting crisis day at the Supreme Court. The justices — over dissents from Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson — vacated the federal district court order blocking Alabama from implementing its 2023 congressional map, which the district court had found violated Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act by intentionally diluting Black voting power. The Court remanded for reconsideration under Louisiana v. Callais, the May 2026 ruling that significantly narrowed VRA Section 2 liability by requiring plaintiffs to show that a "racially neutral" alternative map existed and would have substantially reduced minority dilution. Alabama had argued no such alternative existed under its neutrality criteria. Hours later, Virginia Democrats and Attorney General Jay Jones filed an emergency petition asking the Supreme Court to reinstate a congressional map the Democratic-controlled legislature drew; the Virginia Supreme Court had struck down the constitutional amendment authorizing the legislature to draw it. The Virginia map, if reinstated, could flip as many as four congressional seats from Republican to Democrat for November 2026.

**Why it matters:** The Alabama order means a map a federal court found intentionally racially discriminatory will be used in 2026, likely reducing majority-Black congressional districts from two to one. The Callais remand signals how far the Court intends to let its new VRA framework reshape redistricting litigation — the "racially neutral alternative" requirement effectively shifts the burden to plaintiffs in a way civil rights lawyers say is nearly impossible to satisfy. The Virginia application raises distinct questions about state constitutional delegation of redistricting power and federal court review of state supreme court interpretations. Together, these orders confirm redistricting will remain a live Supreme Court issue through November, with outcomes shaping House control.

## [cybersecurity] Foxconn confirms Nitrogen ransomware attack on North American factories, third major breach since 2020

**Source:** [The Record](https://therecord.media/foxconn-confirms-cyberattack-north-american-factories)

**Lead:** Foxconn, the $258 billion Taiwanese electronics manufacturer that produces hardware for Apple, Google, Microsoft, and Cisco, confirmed Monday that the Nitrogen ransomware gang struck multiple North American factories beginning Friday, claiming to have stolen 8 terabytes of technical data.

**Context:** Foxconn disclosed Monday that a ransomware attack beginning Friday had disrupted network connectivity at multiple North American facilities in Wisconsin, Ohio, Texas, Virginia, Indiana, and Mexico, forcing some workers to resort to paper and pen. The Nitrogen ransomware gang claimed responsibility, asserting they stole 8 terabytes of data comprising "millions of files that include technical information from several prominent tech firms." The Nitrogen strain is believed derived from defunct Conti ransomware code, broadly associated with Russian-affiliated criminal networks. Foxconn confirmed production resumed after incident response but declined to specify the number of affected sites. This is the third significant ransomware incident against Foxconn since 2020.

**Why it matters:** The attack carries significant supply chain risk: 8 terabytes of alleged technical data from Foxconn's clients — including Apple, Google, and Microsoft — raises immediate questions about whether proprietary designs or manufacturing process data are now in criminal hands. A pattern of three successful attacks on the same company signals persistent security deficiencies or deliberate targeting for supply chain access. Nitrogen's Conti-derived code places this in Russian-affiliated criminal infrastructure, and US government agencies may examine whether defense-adjacent data flowed through affected facilities under supply-chain security directives.

## [global politics] CBO puts Trump's Golden Dome missile defense at $1.2 trillion over 20 years, nearly seven times the stated cost

**Source:** [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/13/g-s1-121780/trumps-golden-dome-cost)

**Lead:** The nonpartisan Congressional Budget Office estimates that Trump's "Golden Dome" space-based missile defense initiative will cost $1.2 trillion over 20 years, nearly seven times the $175 billion figure Trump originally cited and six times the $185 billion allocated in the FY2027 defense budget.

**Context:** The CBO released its analysis May 12, calling it "one illustrative approach" because the Defense Department lacks sufficient technical specifics for a concrete deployment estimate — nearly a year after the executive order establishing the program. Trump initially stated the cost at $175 billion and pledged full operational capability before January 2029; the CBO now puts acquisition costs alone at just over $1 trillion, total 20-year cost at $1.2 trillion. Congress approved approximately $24 billion through a tax and spending package; the FY2027 budget proposes an additional $185 billion. The system aims for multilayer ground- and space-based interception across all missile flight stages.

**Why it matters:** At $1.2 trillion, the program would dwarf all prior US defense acquisitions (the F-35 cost ~$400 billion) and consume a significant fraction of discretionary federal spending if funded to completion. Existing US missile defense systems struggle against ICBMs deployed by Russia and China — the scenarios the program targets. The CBO's inability to model a specific plan because the Pentagon lacks one raises programmatic readiness concerns. Defense contractors including Northrop Grumman, L3Harris, and Lockheed Martin stand to benefit, and the political economy of such a large program makes cancellation extremely difficult once contracts are distributed across states. If implemented at scale, the program would reshape federal fiscal priorities for decades.

## [cybersecurity] Patch Tuesday May 2026: critical Windows Netlogon overflow, 118 Microsoft fixes, AI-driven detection fuels record patch volumes

**Lead:** Microsoft patched 118 security vulnerabilities in May 2026's Patch Tuesday including a critical stack-based buffer overflow in Windows Netlogon that grants SYSTEM privileges on domain controllers without user interaction, with no zero-days exploited in the wild for the first time in nearly two years, as Anthropic's Project Glasswing AI program drove 271 of Firefox 150's vulnerability fixes.

**Context:** May's Patch Tuesday was notable for extraordinary volume and — unusually — no exploited zero-days. Microsoft's 118 fixes were headlined by CVE-2026-41089 (critical Netlogon buffer overflow, unauthenticated SYSTEM privileges on domain controllers), CVE-2026-41096 (DNS client RCE), and CVE-2026-41103 (credential impersonation via forged tokens). Apple patched 52 iOS 15 flaws, Google pushed 127 Chrome fixes, Oracle released 450 patches quarterly, and Firefox 150 was patched for vulnerabilities — 271 of which Krebs reports were discovered by Anthropic's "Project Glasswing," a Claude-based automated vulnerability detection program.

**Why it matters:** CVE-2026-41089 requires emergency priority patching in any organization running on-premises Active Directory: domain controllers are the backbone of enterprise Windows authentication, and unauthenticated SYSTEM access without user interaction is the capability ransomware and nation-state actors need for full-network lateral movement. The 271 Firefox fixes from a single AI program in a single release cycle signals how dramatically AI is accelerating vulnerability discovery — for defenders and inevitably for attackers. The absence of zero-days may be temporary; it may reflect adversaries holding exploits or AI defenses finding them first. Organizations should prioritize CVE-2026-41089 for immediate deployment given the severity class.

## [tech law] Congress probes 25 major food retailers on AI-driven surveillance pricing using personal customer data

**Source:** [The Record](https://therecord.media/congressman-launches-inquiry-into-food-retail-surveillance)

**Lead:** Rep. Frank Pallone (D-NJ), ranking member of the House Energy and Commerce Committee, sent letters to Amazon, Walmart, Target, Albertsons, CVS, and 20 other major retailers demanding disclosure of whether they use personal customer data and AI algorithms to set individualized prices.

**Context:** Pallone's inquiry targeted 25 major US food and pharmacy retailers following a January 2025 FTC report documenting widespread surveillance-based dynamic pricing. The practice became publicly visible in New York when disclosure requirements took effect: Target began displaying pop-ups stating prices are "set by an algorithm using your personal data." Retailers collect geolocation, shopping history, demographic, behavioral data, and third-party broker signals to power pricing models. The letters demand disclosure of which data elements are used, whether AI determines prices, whether third-party data is purchased, and what opt-out mechanisms exist.

**Why it matters:** Personalized algorithmic pricing implicates multiple unresolved legal questions: Robinson-Patman Act price discrimination, state consumer protection statutes, and consent requirements under state privacy laws (CPRA, CDPA). Congressional findings could accelerate the FTC's open rulemaking on surveillance pricing or anchor state AG enforcement under unfair business practice statutes. The political dimension is potent: AI-driven individualized pricing is easily frameable as corporate manipulation of financially stressed consumers, a high-salience issue heading into the 2026 midterms, and this inquiry establishes the evidentiary record needed for legislative action.

## [law] Justice Alito says Supreme Court oral arguments have fundamentally changed, calls post-Dobbs security crisis ongoing

**Source:** [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/05/justice-samuel-alito-opens-up-about-oral-arguments-judicial-security-and-his-writing-process/)

**Lead:** Justice Samuel Alito said in a rare extended interview that Supreme Court oral arguments have become "entirely different" since 2006 due to excessive speechifying by justices, described the security crisis following the Dobbs draft leak as ongoing and making judges' work significantly harder, and disclosed he is completing a book on originalism.

**Context:** SCOTUSblog published an extended interview with Alito covering three substantive areas. On oral arguments: he described a shift since 2006 toward justices "speechifying" — using questions to make statements rather than probe issues — conducted an experiment refraining from questions himself (finding it "unbelievably boring"), but concluded arguments retain value despite limited influence on outcomes. On the Dobbs leak: described the May 2022 disclosure of his draft Dobbs opinion as creating an ongoing security crisis for the federal judiciary, making judges' work "significantly harder," though he declined to identify the leaker. On writing: law clerks produce outlines and drafts he edits thoroughly, with concurrences and dissents handled more personally; finding law school graduates who write well is "the greatest challenge."

**Why it matters:** Alito's critique of oral argument culture is consistent with empirical research showing Supreme Court questions have grown longer and less case-specific over two decades; if shared by other justices, it could prompt format reforms. His continued public emphasis on the Dobbs leak signals an unresolved internal wound and may foreshadow continued institutional defensiveness about transparency. His forthcoming book distinguishing judicial from scholarly originalism will become a key reference as the Court continues to reshape constitutional law in administrative, religious freedom, and executive power cases. As one of the Court's most prolific and influential writers, his candid disclosures about internal practices are exceptionally rare and substantively significant.
